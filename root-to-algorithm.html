Root to Algorithm

Root to Algorithm explores the relationship between nature, identity and machine intelligence. Beginning with interconnected root systems and culminating in immersive data environments, the series examines how biological and digital networks increasingly shape contemporary existence. Combining photography, digital manipulation and AI-inspired visual language, the works question where the boundaries between nature, humanity and technology now reside.

Residual Language

Digital mixed media

Constructed from fragmented text and digital artefacts, the portrait explores how identity is increasingly encoded, processed and reconstructed through digital systems, leaving algorithmic traces that persist beyond lived experience.

Networked Consciousness

Digital mixed media

Streams of light and digital pathways create an immersive landscape where information flows beyond human perception, inviting reflection on how technological systems increasingly shape identity, behaviour and everyday life.

Root Protocol

Digital mixed media

Tree roots merge with circuit-like structures, dissolving distinctions between natural growth and computational design. The work proposes that intelligence emerges through the continuous exchange between nature and technology.

Predictive Self

Digital mixed media

Through facial recognition and biometric mapping, the portrait questions how algorithms increasingly participate in defining identity, translating individuals into data, probabilities and machine interpretation.

Algorithm of Light

Digital mixed media

Binary code and geometric structures emerge from the forest, transforming an ancient landscape into a meeting point between organic ecosystems and computational futures.

Neural Flock

Digital mixed media

A coordinated flock of birds becomes a living neural network, suggesting that the organisational principles of artificial intelligence have long existed within the natural world.

Encoded Reverie

Digital mixed media

Suspended between introspection and digital cognition, the figure reflects on how thoughts, memories and identity are increasingly shaped by artificial intelligence and interconnected technologies.

Signal Lost

Digital mixed media

Surrounded by messages, notifications and virtual conversations, the figure remains emotionally isolated. The work reflects on the paradox of constant connectivity and the search for genuine human presence.
